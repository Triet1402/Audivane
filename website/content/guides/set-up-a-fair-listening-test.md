Audivane makes the audio pair fair: same source, alignment, gain matching,
verified delivery. The other half of fairness is the physical setup.

## Fix the output path

Pick one output device and keep it for the whole test.

- Disable OS audio "enhancements", equalizers and loudness normalization —
  they process everything you hear and sit inside the result.
- Wired output removes one variable. Bluetooth adds an OS and codec path your
  test cannot see; treat the whole chain as the thing under test, and repeat
  after pairing changes or driver updates.
- Keep volume fixed between sessions. The app gain-matches the pair, so
  neither side is louder; your overall level should still be the same across
  runs.

## Choose material that suits the question

- Use several familiar tracks, not one. A single file turns the test into a
  drill for one artifact.
- Dense, full-range material with vocals and transients gives more to compare
  than silence-heavy recordings.
- If the file is already an MP3, both chains degrade the same lossy source.
  Comparing formats means starting from a clean source.

## Control yourself

- Decide the trial count before you start, when you have no opinion about the
  outcome. Stopping when it "feels conclusive" is the easiest way to fool a
  fixed-trial test — the [results guide](/guides/understanding-your-results/)
  covers why.
- Test when you are rested, in a quiet room. A failed run in bad conditions is
  a scheduling fact, not a finding.
- Listen alone. An ABX result is about one pair of ears.

## What fairness does not mean

A fair setup and a passing preparation do not certify audibility or codec
conformance. They mean the comparison was constructed honestly, on your
machine, under the conditions you chose.
