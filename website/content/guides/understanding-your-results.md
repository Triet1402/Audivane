A completed Audivane run reports answers against the trial count you committed
to, with binomial statistics.

## Every trial is a coin flip you might beat

Each trial asks "Is X A or B?", so pure guessing is right about half the time.
That is the baseline every result is measured against:

- 7 trials, 7 correct: pure guessing reaches that about **0.8%** of the time.
- 7 trials, at least 6 correct: about **6.3%** of the time.
- 10 trials, at least 8 correct: about **5.5%** of the time.
- 20 trials, at least 15 correct: about **2.1%** of the time.

Audivane reports the actual binomial probability and confidence interval for
*your* run rather than a badge.

## Why the trial count is fixed in advance

The plan locks when the first trial is staged, and exactly that count is
scored. If you keep going until you "sound convincing," the coin-flip odds no
longer apply, because you have chosen the stopping point after seeing the
answers.

That is why an early-ended or sealed run is kept visibly distinct from a
completed one and cannot claim a score. If conditions collapse, end the run
and start a fresh one later.

## Choosing N

Common practice is a small fixed count chosen beforehand — something like 7 or
10 trials. More trials separate "I think I heard something" from "I keep
beating chance," at the cost of fatigue; pick N while you are neutral about
the outcome.

## Validity is not audibility

A completed, valid run establishes that *you, on this machine, with this
material, beat chance at the committed count.* It does not support saying
"the codec is worse" — other hardware, material and listeners can legitimately
get different results. Nor does preparation validity certify audibility or
your physical device chain.

If a completed run beats chance, the honest summary is: *"Under these
conditions, I could tell the two chains apart at better than chance."* Run it
again with different material before saying anything louder than that — and
try the reverse: test a chain against itself (the same settings on both sides)
and confirm the score drops to chance.
